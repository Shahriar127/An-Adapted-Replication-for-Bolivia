## Conventions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Metrics | Published Table 2 (p. 534). TA = (TP+TN)/N. **PA, UC, LE and BPAC all use the denominator TP+FN** (true very poor). BPAC = PA − \|UC − LE\|. |
| Poverty line | 189.09 Bs/person/month = P24.2 of the full sample, unless stated otherwise |
| Published protocol | Train and test = two independent N/2 draws *with replacement* (p. 539); ~40% of unique test households also in training |
| Disjoint protocol | Our adaptation: random 50/50 partition, untouched test set |
| Primary | Seed 42; mean of 1,000 test-sample bootstrap resamples, [95% percentile CI] |
| Repeated | 30 splits per protocol (seeds 1000–1029); mean (SD) of point estimates |
| SE | Stochastic ensemble: candidate (RF or QRF q = 0.01–0.99) with the best **out-of-bag** BPAC on the training sample |
| CV-QR | Linear QR, quantile chosen by 500 × 3-fold CV inside the training sample |
| IRIS | Linear QR at the fixed published quantile 0.42 |

**Evidence labels:** **FINAL** = frozen pipeline (`run_all.sh`). **FINAL-AUDIT** = separate documented audit (`run_audits.sh` or an `audit_*` folder) that does not change the frozen pipeline. Single-split results are marked as such.

Paper-ready versions of every table: `final_manuscript/tables/` (generated by `make_tables.py`). Figures: `final_manuscript/figures/`.

## 6. RQ5 — Forest flexibility or quantile tuning?

**FINAL-AUDIT. Seed 42 and a 3-split pilot.** Tables S3 and S4. Sources: `results/cv_qr/paired_comparisons.csv`, `audit_2026-11-04_repeated_split/outputs/pilot_per_split_results.csv`, `audit_2026-11-04_step5_quantile_path/outputs/`.

Paired BPAC differences on identical test households, [95% CI]:

| Contrast | Published protocol | Disjoint protocol | Published Table 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| CV-QR − IRIS | +0.037 [−0.056, +0.126] | +0.116 [−0.026, +0.226] | +0.086 |
| SE − IRIS | +0.067 [+0.002, +0.137] | +0.120 [−0.011, +0.222] | +0.009 (n.s.) |
| CV-QR − SE | −0.030 [−0.109, +0.065] | −0.004 [−0.053, +0.056] | +0.078 |

* **Against the fixed benchmark, 30 splits (FINAL):** SE − IRIS is positive in 30/30 splits under both protocols (mean +0.196 published, +0.162 disjoint).
* **Three-split pilot (published protocol):** CV-QR − QRF = −0.039, −0.068, −0.038. Each CI reaches or crosses zero. The pilot is confounded with overlap inflation (~+0.035). The planned 10-split study was **not run**.
* **Quantile path** (disjoint, seed 42; Figure 2):
  * At each method's training-selected q: QRF 0.642 vs linear QR 0.629 (+0.013).
  * At the common q = 0.42: QRF leads by 0.078.
  * At q = 0.36: linear QR leads by 0.110.
* **Conclusion:** much of the QRF advantage over IRIS is an advantage over a *fixed* quantile. Whether the forest adds accuracy beyond a tuned linear model is **open**.

## 7. Replication discrepancy A8 (unresolved)

**FINAL-AUDIT. Published protocol, seed 42 + 20 splits.** Manuscript Table 3 → `tables/table3_a8_audit.csv`. Sources: `results/a8_audit/`.

* Our SE BPAC is a mean of 0.685 over 20 splits, against the published 0.502.
* **Ruled out:**
  * implementation: an R `quantregForest`/`randomForest` emulation, validated to 1.6e-12. R's node-size rule *raises* BPAC by about 0.04;
  * split variation: the published value lies **3.0–10.0** split SDs below ours in every configuration;
  * duplicated households in OOB selection, feature coding, log target and the P24.03 line: each changes BPAC by ≤ 0.003.
* **Confirmed:** our welfare-variable distribution differs from the authors'.
  * Rates at half and double the line: ours 6.07% / 57.00%, published 4.92% / 62.26%.
  * None of the 9 aggregates in the survey file matches.
  * The in-sample refit of row 5 matches on PA and UC but not on LE (0.250 vs 0.337).
* **Not established:** that the data difference *causes* the forest gap.
